Know if you can fly your drone there

Kestrel Drone is a free airspace and preflight app for drone pilots, recreational and Part 107. Tap a spot to see if you can fly, and why.

Open the app Install on your phone or computer

One answer for every spot

Tap the map or use your location. Kestrel Drone gives one of four answers, with the reasons:

  • Clear to fly. Uncontrolled airspace, up to 400 ft above the ground.
  • LAANC authorization needed, with the most LAANC can approve in that grid square.
  • Authorization needed through FAA DroneZone, where LAANC isn't available.
  • Restricted. A TFR, security restriction, prohibited area or national park.

Everything that limits a drone, on one map

  • Class B, C, D and E airspace to the surface
  • FAA UAS facility map ceilings for each grid square
  • TFRs, including stadium and sporting event restrictions
  • National Security UAS Flight Restrictions
  • Prohibited and restricted areas
  • National parks, where drones can't take off or land
  • FRIAs for Remote ID, recreational flying sites, airports and heliports

LAANC in the app you already use

When a spot needs LAANC, Kestrel Drone opens the FAA-approved LAANC provider you choose, Aloft, AirHub Portal or AutoPylot, and copies the location and altitude so you only paste them. Your request goes through that provider.

Where LAANC isn't available, it links to FAA DroneZone instead.

Weather for drones

  • Wind at the surface and at about 260 and 390 ft, with gusts
  • Temperature, cloud base, visibility and rain chance
  • The Kp index, which tells you when GPS may drift
  • Sunrise, sunset and civil twilight, for Part 107 lighting rules
